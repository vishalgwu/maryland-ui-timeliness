Unemployment insurance · Maryland · Sep 2026

When a paycheck stops, the first payment matters most.

Federal standards say 87% of first unemployment payments should reach workers within 21 days. In Maryland the figure is 72.5%. This is an independent, respectful look at what the numbers say, how Maryland compares with every state, and the work already under way to close the gap.

      06 · The method

      How the numbers were made, and checked.

      All public data. Fully reproducible. Open source.

      The measure

      The on-time rate is the share of first payments made within 21 days of the first compensable week, from US Department of Labor file ETA 9050 (intrastate and interstate). The 12-month rate adds payments first, then divides.

      The check

      The file's delay bands add up to its total, and the annual results match the Department's published chart: Maryland 69.3% and US 83.4% for 2025, plus the reported 14.1% low in June 2020.

      The limits

      The Department says backdated claims can make the federal measure look worse, and public data cannot separate that from agency delay. Correlations are descriptive. The forecast is illustrative. Department figures were read from its slides.
